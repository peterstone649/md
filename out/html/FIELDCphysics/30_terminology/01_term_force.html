Force Characteristics

Fundamental Properties

  • Vector Nature: Force has both magnitude and direction, requiring vector mathematics for complete description
  • Superposition Principle: Multiple forces acting on an object add vectorially to produce a net force
  • Action-Reaction Pairs: Every force has an equal and opposite reaction force (Newton's Third Law)
  • Conservation: Force is related to the conservation of momentum through Newton's Second Law

Force Types

  • Contact Forces: Forces requiring physical contact (friction, normal force, tension, applied force)
  • Field Forces: Forces acting at a distance through fields (gravitational, electromagnetic, nuclear forces)
  • Conservative Forces: Forces where work done is path-independent (gravity, spring force, electrostatic)
  • Non-Conservative Forces: Forces where work depends on path (friction, air resistance, viscous drag)

Force Relationships

  • Newton's Second Law: F = ma (force equals mass times acceleration)
  • Momentum Relationship: F = dp/dt (force equals rate of change of momentum)
  • Work-Energy Theorem: W = ∫F·ds (work done by force equals change in kinetic energy)
  • Potential Energy: F = -∇U (force equals negative gradient of potential energy)
