Ethics Branch

Overview

The Ethics branch explores moral philosophy and the principles of right and wrong conduct, examining theories of value, virtue, justice, and moral reasoning. This branch addresses fundamental questions about how we should live and what constitutes a good life.

Core Concepts

Normative Ethics - Theories of Right Action

  • Consequentialism: Moral evaluation based on outcomes
  • Deontology: Moral evaluation based on rules and duties
  • Virtue Ethics: Moral evaluation based on character
  • Care Ethics: Moral evaluation based on relationships

Meta-Ethics - Nature of Moral Judgments

  • Moral realism vs. anti-realism
  • Cognitivism vs. non-cognitivism
  • Naturalism vs. non-naturalism
  • Relativism vs. universalism

Applied Ethics - Moral Issues in Practice

  • Bioethics: Medical and biological moral issues
  • Environmental Ethics: Human-nature moral relationships
  • Business Ethics: Commerce and organizational morality
  • AI Ethics: Artificial intelligence moral considerations

Value Theory - Nature of Moral Value

  • Intrinsic vs. instrumental value
  • Objective vs. subjective value
  • Moral vs. non-moral value
  • Value pluralism and conflict

Key Theories

Classical Ethical Theories

  • Aristotelian Virtue Ethics: Excellence of character and practical wisdom
  • Kantian Deontology: Universal moral law and categorical imperative
  • Utilitarian Consequentialism: Greatest happiness principle
  • Stoic Ethics: Living according to nature and reason

Modern Ethical Theories

  • Rawls' Justice as Fairness: Social contract theory of justice
  • Care Ethics: Ethics of care and relationships (Gilligan)
  • Contractarianism: Moral principles as social agreements
  • Existential Ethics: Individual responsibility and authenticity
