# 4. Logic (DEF_FOR_LOGIC) **[PRIO: HIGH]**

*   **Logic:** The branch of philosophy that studies the principles of valid reasoning and argumentation, examining formal and informal logic, deductive and inductive reasoning, and providing tools for clear thinking and sound argumentation.
*   **Description:** Logic investigates the fundamental principles of correct reasoning, examining how we can draw valid conclusions from premises and construct sound arguments. It provides systematic methods for evaluating arguments, identifying fallacies, and ensuring rational consistency in thought and communication. Logic serves as the foundation for all rational inquiry and critical thinking.

*   **Type Classification:** PHILOSOPHICAL / FORMAL

## Logical Foundations

### **Types of Logic**
- **Formal Logic:** Study of logical form and validity using symbolic notation
- **Informal Logic:** Study of argumentation and reasoning in natural language
- **Mathematical Logic:** Application of logic to mathematics and computation
- **Philosophical Logic:** Logic applied to philosophical problems and concepts
- **Computational Logic:** Logic in computer science and artificial intelligence

### **Logical Systems**
- **Propositional Logic:** Logic of statements and their truth values
- **Predicate Logic:** Logic of properties, relations, and quantification
- **Modal Logic:** Logic of possibility, necessity, and other modalities
- **Temporal Logic:** Logic of time and temporal relations
- **Deontic Logic:** Logic of obligation, permission, and prohibition

### **Logical Operations**
- **Conjunction (AND):** Logical combination requiring both premises
- **Disjunction (OR):** Logical combination requiring at least one premise
- **Negation (NOT):** Logical operation reversing truth values
- **Implication (IF-THEN):** Logical relation of conditional dependence
- **Biconditional (IF AND ONLY IF):** Logical relation of mutual dependence

### **Logical Reasoning**
- **Deduction:** Reasoning from general premises to specific conclusions
- **Induction:** Reasoning from specific observations to general conclusions
- **Abduction:** Reasoning to the best explanation for observed facts
- **Analogy:** Reasoning based on similarities between cases
- **Causal Reasoning:** Reasoning about cause and effect relationships

## Logical Theories

### **Classical Logic**
- **Aristotelian Logic:** Syllogistic reasoning and categorical propositions
- **Stoic Logic:** Propositional logic and truth-functional connectives
- **Medieval Logic:** Development of terminist logic and supposition theory
- **Modern Logic:** Frege's predicate logic and Russell's theory of descriptions
- **Contemporary Logic:** Gödel's incompleteness theorems and Turing's computability

### **Non-Classical Logics**
- **Intuitionistic Logic:** Logic without the law of excluded middle
- **Many-Valued Logic:** Logic with more than two truth values
- **Fuzzy Logic:** Logic dealing with degrees of truth
- **Paraconsistent Logic:** Logic that can handle contradictions
- **Relevance Logic:** Logic requiring premise relevance to conclusions

### **Applied Logic**
- **Computational Logic:** Logic in programming and computer science
- **Legal Logic:** Logic in legal reasoning and argumentation
- **Scientific Logic:** Logic in hypothesis testing and theory formation
- **Everyday Logic:** Logic in practical reasoning and decision-making
- **Critical Thinking:** Logic in evaluating arguments and claims

### **Logical Methods**
- **Formal Proofs:** Rigorous demonstrations of logical validity
- **Argument Analysis:** Systematic evaluation of argument structure
- **Fallacy Detection:** Identification of common reasoning errors
- **Logical Modeling:** Representation of reasoning processes
- **Automated Reasoning:** Computer-assisted logical inference
