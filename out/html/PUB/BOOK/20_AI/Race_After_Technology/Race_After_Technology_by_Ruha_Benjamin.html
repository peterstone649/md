Overview

Race After Technology: Abolitionist Tools for the New Jim Code is a powerful and incisive work by Ruha Benjamin, published in 2019, that exposes how technology is not neutral but actively reinforces racial hierarchies and perpetuates systemic racism. Benjamin, a sociologist and associate professor at Princeton University, introduces the concept of the "New Jim Code" to describe how coded bias in technology creates new forms of racial discrimination that are often invisible and automated.

Conclusion

Race After Technology stands as one of the most important books on the intersection of technology and racial justice, providing a devastating critique of how technology reinforces racial hierarchies and perpetuates systemic racism. Ruha Benjamin combines rigorous research with compelling analysis to expose the hidden costs of "neutral" technology in America.

The book's core message is both urgent and empowering: technology is not neutral, and we have the power to demand more just and equitable systems. Benjamin demonstrates that the choices we make about technology reflect our values and priorities as a society.

In the face of increasing technological surveillance and control, Benjamin's work serves as both a warning and a call to action, reminding us that technology should serve racial justice, not perpetuate historical patterns of oppression.

The fight against technological racism is not just about fixing algorithms, but about transforming the power structures that shape technology development and deployment. 🏛️✊💻
