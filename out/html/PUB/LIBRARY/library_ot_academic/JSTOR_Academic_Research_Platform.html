JSTOR: A Comprehensive Analysis of the Academic Research Platform

Overview

JSTOR (Journal Storage) is a digital library founded in 1995 that provides access to academic journals, books, and primary sources across various disciplines. Originally conceived as a solution to the problem of storing and accessing back issues of academic journals, JSTOR has evolved into one of the most comprehensive and widely used academic research platforms globally.

Historical Development

Founding and Early Years (1995-2000)

  • Founded: 1995 by Mike Rossiter at ITHAKA
  • Initial Mission: Digitize back issues of academic journals to save library space
  • First Content: Started with economics journals, expanded to other disciplines
  • Technology: Initially used static image files (TIFF format) for journal articles

Expansion and Evolution (2000-2010)

  • Content Growth: Expanded from 15 journals to thousands of titles
  • Format Evolution: Moved from static images to searchable, full-text content
  • Subject Diversification: Added humanities, social sciences, and sciences
  • Partnerships: Established relationships with major academic publishers

Modern Era (2010-Present)

  • Platform Enhancement: Improved search capabilities and user interface
  • Mobile Access: Developed mobile applications for iOS and Android
  • Open Access: Launched initiatives for open access content
  • Data Analytics: Added tools for research impact and usage analytics
